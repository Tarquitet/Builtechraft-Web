## Tarquitet

**Tarquitet** founded Builtechraft and leads its technical direction. He has played Minecraft since 2013–2014. His approach brings creativity, engineering, and a stable semi-vanilla experience together.

<WikiAccordion title="Minecraft as a laboratory">

Architecture is one of his hobbies, reflected in projects with scale, composition, and spaces designed to be explored.

Tarquitet is a **multimedia engineer**. In Minecraft, that perspective combines systems, design, and interactive experiences through redstone, farms, responsible automation, and durable infrastructure.

</WikiAccordion>

## Why Minecraft?

Minecraft is a natural medium for this mix of interests. It has no single fixed goal: players can design spaces, build systems, and turn technical ideas into shared experiences.

<WikiCallout type="info" title="Two disciplines, one world">

- **Architecture** brings scale, composition, and flow.
- **Multimedia engineering** adds interaction, logic, and visual tools.
- Redstone, datapacks, and mods connect both disciplines.

</WikiCallout>

Other games provide defined experiences. Minecraft lets players create the rules, places, and tools. That is why Builtechraft treats the game as a creative and technical workshop.

## His work at Builtechraft

Tarquitet designs and maintains the systems behind the Creative and Survival worlds:

- Tests mods, datapacks, and settings before applying them.
- Improves performance, security, and backups.
- Builds tools that make exploration, construction, and collaboration easier.

<WikiCallout type="warn" title="The philosophy">

The goal is not to replace Minecraft. It is to preserve its mechanics and remove unnecessary friction for the community.

</WikiCallout>

## A shared vision

Builtechraft combines artistic builds with technical projects. Every system should serve the players and respect the world everyone has built together.
