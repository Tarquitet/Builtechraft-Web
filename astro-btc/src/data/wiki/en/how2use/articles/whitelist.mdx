The **whitelist** protects our worlds and the community's work. Request access on Discord before trying to join.

## Why the server is semi-private

Builtechraft originally ran as a fully public server. Anyone with the IP address could join, explore, and build. Over time, however, we found that some people abused that freedom to damage other players' work and disrupt the calm environment we wanted to create.

To protect our players' efforts, megabuilds, and engineering systems, we decided to make the server **semi-private**.

Today, you must be registered in our database to enter any of our worlds. If your username is not on the whitelist, the server will automatically reject your connection.

## Waiting time

We know you are eager to play, but please be patient. The process is manual, and administrators have other responsibilities such as work and school, so approval is not instant.

<WikiCallout type="ok" title="Usually under 24 hours">

Your username is usually added to the whitelist in **less than a day**. Once an administrator adds you, they will notify you directly on Discord so you know you can join.

</WikiCallout>

## After approval

Once you are told that you are on the whitelist, you can connect and load the world. You may notice that your character is frozen and cannot interact with anything.

<WikiCallout type="info" title="This is not lag">

This happens because of our **internal password login system**. Register the first time you join to protect your identity and start playing.

</WikiCallout>

## Frequently asked questions

<WikiAccordion title="What if I forget my password?">

Submit a request in the Discord forum. Staff can help reset it after verifying your identity.

</WikiAccordion>

<WikiAccordion title="Can I change my username?">

Changing your name may affect your access, inventory, and whitelist. Submit a request before joining with your new name.

</WikiAccordion>

If you are not on the whitelist, the server will automatically reject you. Request access on Discord and wait for approval.
